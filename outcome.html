Looking back ~25s

What worked

Which of our planned tactics actually landed — staying calm under pressure, listening before we pushed our numbers, building on their ideas rather than just selling ours? Which of our prepared options did Council and the Park actually engage with?

Looking back ~25s

What didn't

Where did we react instead of respond? Did we hold our reserve positions too long, or open too low or too high? Where did the other parties out-negotiate us, and why?

Looking back ~20s

Sources of conflict

The core conflict was contested causation — who's really polluting — layered onto a shared-cost problem.

Did we resolve that with evidence (the hydrology assessment), or did it just get traded away? What other conflicts of interest surfaced — budget vs. urgency vs. reputation?

Looking back ~20s

Was the outcome fair?

Fair to us isn't the same test as fair overall.

Judge it on whether cost actually followed who caused the problem and who benefits, backed by evidence rather than assumption — not just on whether it beat our own walk-away point.

Bigger picture · 1 of 2 ~30s

What this taught us

  • Personal views & professional judgement. Did our own instincts — reluctance to be blamed, protectiveness of the Cemetery's finances — shape how hard we pushed, in ways a neutral planner might not have?
  • Ethical responsibilities. We opened by contesting the pollution evidence. Was that a legitimate negotiating position, or does it sit uneasily with dealing with environmental impact in good faith? Where's the line?
Bigger picture · 2 of 2 ~30s

What this taught us

  • Communicating across perspectives. How did we adapt our language for Council (budget and political framing) versus National Park (ecological and safety framing)? Did our written proposal read as collaborative, or as an opening bid dressed up as consensus?
  • Evidence-based argument. Did we actually get the independent hydrology assessment moving, or did we just propose it and let the point drop? What would a more evidence-led version of our case have looked like?

Our fifteen minutes move straight through this site, in order: the case, our preparation, our proposal, the 29 July exchange, the outcome, what we'd do differently. Three tags run across every page to mark what that actually means. Presentation copy option — the slide deck at the top of each page. Logistics — who was in the room, who did which task, sign-off records, who presents which segment: organisational only, doesn't factor into the 15 minutes at all. Each page is laid out in that order too — the deck first (white), supporting detail next (black), logistics last (white) — so presenting is arrow through the deck, then scroll on if the room asks for more.
