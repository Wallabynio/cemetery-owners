Looking back Presented live · ~90s

What worked, what didn't

What worked

Which Breakthrough Strategy moves actually landed (going to the balcony, reframing, building a golden bridge)? Which of our prepared options or standards did Council/Park engage with?

What didn't

Where did we react instead of respond? Did we hold our reserve positions too long, or open too low/high? Where did the other parties out-negotiate us, and why?

Sources of conflict

The core conflict was contested causation (who's really polluting) layered onto a shared-cost problem. Did we resolve that with evidence (hydrology) or did it just get traded away? What other conflicts of interest surfaced — budget vs. urgency vs. reputation?

Was the outcome fair?

Judge the final split against the standards we named in Preparation (polluter pays, beneficiary pays, independent evidence) rather than just against our own BATNA. Fair to us isn't the same test as fair overall.

Bigger picture Presented live · ~60s

What this taught us

  • Personal views & professional judgement. Did our own instincts (e.g. reluctance to be blamed, protectiveness of the Cemetery's finances) shape how hard we pushed, in ways a neutral planner might not have?
  • Ethical responsibilities. We opened by contesting the pollution evidence — was that a legitimate negotiating position, or does it sit uneasily with dealing with environmental impact in good faith? Where's the line?
  • Communicating across perspectives. How did we adapt our language for Council (budget/political framing) versus National Park (ecological/safety framing)? Did our written proposal read as collaborative, or as an opening bid dressed up as consensus?
  • Evidence-based argument. Did we actually get the independent hydrology assessment moving, or did we just propose it and let the point drop? What would a more evidence-led version of our case have looked like?
