Our plan going in

Tactics, given all three letters

  • Name the common ground first. Council's "ownership, responsibility, benefit and capacity" test and the Park's "commensurate with benefit" are both proportionality arguments — the same standard we'd set for ourselves.
  • Offer the hydrology assessment ourselves. Council's own non-negotiable asks for stormwater to be "effectively managed" and access "independently verified" — so offer the evidence up front rather than waiting to be pushed into it.
  • Reframe, don't repeat, on parking. Council's "parking operations, costs and use of revenue" is broader than the line we drew. Clarify what's off the table (sharing the $0.3M meter cost) versus what isn't (what the revenue funds).
  • Nobody named a number. That confirmed keeping our range in reserve — but it also means the first figure spoken in the room carries real weight.
  • The Park never mentioned stormwater. The pollution pressure sits with Council's letter, not the Park's — a different room than we prepared for.

Breakthrough Strategy — the five moves

The framework we kept open on the day.

1 · Go to the balcony

Don't react. If we feel the urge to strike back or give in, pause or take a break before responding.

2 · Step to their side

Don't argue. Paraphrase Council and the Park back to them, and find real agreement before pushing our numbers.

3 · Reframe

Don't reject. Turn "why is this our fault" into "what would a fair split look like" or "how do we test the pollution claim."

4 · Build a golden bridge

Don't push. Ask for their ideas first; put the pond up as theirs to shape, not just ours to sell.

5 · Use power to educate

Don't escalate. Note calmly what happens without a deal — Council could compel us anyway, worse for everyone. Information, not threat.

Watch for

Stone walls ("that's non-negotiable"), attacks ("you caused this") and selective facts. Name it calmly instead of reacting.
