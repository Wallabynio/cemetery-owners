Breakthrough Strategy — five moves

Five moves, in order, for when the room gets stuck. Keep this open during the session. (Ury, W. 1991. Getting Past No: Negotiating with Difficult People. Bantam Books, pp.176–181.)

1 · Don't react — go to the balcony

Watch for the urge to strike back, give in or walk off. If we feel it, pause, stay quiet, or take a short break before responding.

2 · Don't argue — step to their side

Listen, paraphrase Council and the Park's position back to them, and find real points of agreement before we push our numbers.

3 · Don't reject — reframe

Turn "why is this our fault" into "what would a fair split look like for everyone" or "how do we actually test the pollution claim." Treat a flat "no" as a starting point to work with, not a wall to push against.

4 · Don't push — build a golden bridge

Ask Council and the Park for their ideas before we present ours as final. Put the detention pond up as theirs to shape, not just ours to sell. Go step by step — don't force a final number early.

5 · Don't escalate — use power to educate

If needed, calmly note what happens without a deal — Council could compel us anyway, worse for everyone. Frame it as information, not a threat. Aim for a workable result, not a win.

Watch for their tactics

Stone walls ("that's non-negotiable"), attacks ("you caused this") and tricks (selective facts). Name it calmly instead of reacting.
