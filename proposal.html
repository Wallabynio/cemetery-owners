Council — Position, Negotiables and Non-negotiables

Position

  • The road, access, pedestrian safety, and stormwater issues need to be addressed.
  • Council cannot fund the full project within its current budget and would require approximately five years to fund the works alone.
  • Costs and responsibilities should be allocated fairly according to ownership, responsibility, benefit and capacity to contribute.

Negotiables

  • Funding and contribution arrangements.
  • Scope, design, timing and staging of the works, provided essential risks are addressed.
  • Parking operations, costs and use of revenue.
  • Monitoring and reporting arrangements.

Non-negotiables

  • Council will not bear the full cost of the works; all three parties must make meaningful and proportionate contributions.
  • Emergency access must be independently verified, safe pedestrian access delivered, and stormwater pollution effectively managed.
  • Council retains control over public roads and parking regulation.
  • The agreement must clearly define responsibilities and governance arrangements and provide a better outcome than Council's five-year alternative.

What we'd reconsider now that all three replies are in

We wrote our letter before seeing theirs. Now that all three are on the table, here's what changes — and what we'd still hold to.

  • Council's non-negotiables already ask for what we held in reserve. "Emergency access must be independently verified" and stormwater "effectively managed" line up closely with the independent hydrology assessment we planned to raise only if the room stalled. Worth offering it ourselves early on 29 July rather than waiting to be pushed into it.
  • The pressure we expected didn't come from where we expected it. National Park's own proposal never raised the stormwater/pollution issue at all — see how our position took shape for why we'd read that as them under-using their brief. It means Council's non-negotiables carry the "who's polluting" pressure, not the Park's — a different room than we'd planned for.
  • Council's "parking operations, costs and use of revenue" is broader than the line we drew. We opened with a flat refusal to share the $0.3M meter cost. That's still our non-negotiable — but it's worth listening for whether Council is angling at something narrower, like what the revenue funds, rather than a straight cost-share ask.
  • Nobody named a number. That confirms our plan to keep our own range in reserve rather than anchor first on paper — but it also means the first figure spoken in the room, by anyone, will carry real weight.
  • A golden bridge is sitting in both letters. Council's "monitoring and reporting arrangements" and our own pond-monitoring/RFS-registration idea point the same way — worth offering as a shared deliverable for Council and the Park to help shape, not just ours to sell.

The full letter, as sent Supporting detail

Cemetery Owners: Position, Negotiables and Non-negotiables
To the Council negotiation team and National Park negotiation team

Our position, negotiables and non-negotiables are outlined below. We look forward to meeting on Wednesday to discuss a shared response.

Position

There is run off from the road into the park causing biodiversity issues. It is not clear if roads or the cemetery are the cause. The Cemetery has a long standing and ongoing relationship with Council and the National Park and would like to work towards a mutually beneficial outcome. The road in need of repair and widening is beyond our entrance and does not affect us. The National Park is concerned with bushfire and evacuation risks.

Proposal

We propose jointly funding either in kind or financially an on-site detention pond to be built on cemetery grounds to divert run off going onto the road. It would capture and treat run-off from our site before it reaches the Park, double as an irrigation source for us, and — registered as an NSW RFS Static Water Supply — give the Park a firefighting water source on hand during a bushfire. That last point is why it's worth Council and the Park co-funding: it's a shared bushfire-response asset, not just a stormwater fix on our land.

Negotiables

We value our relationships with Council and the Park and are open to discussing viable alternatives.

Non-negotiables

Parking meters are a revenue producing project for council and we will not consider sharing costs for this.

Kind regards,
Istvan, Georgia and Merryn
The Cemetery

Our fallback range Supporting detail
