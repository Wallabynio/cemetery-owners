The full letter, as sent Supporting detail

To: Council negotiation team, National Park negotiation team
From: Cemetery Owners negotiation team
Re: Scenario 1 — Lane Cove National Park access road, North Ryde

How we see the problem

We agree the road needs fixing. The bushfire-evacuation risk on the narrow section is real, and none of us wants the commuter parking or the pollution running into the Park to carry on. We don't think the stormwater pollution can be pinned on the Cemetery alone — general run-off down the road looks like a major contributor too — but we're not interested in a fight about blame. We'd rather put forward something practical that solves the problem for everyone.

A different way to fix the stormwater

Rather than fund the standard $0.5M stormwater works, we propose building an on-site detention pond at the Cemetery. It would capture and treat run-off from our site before it reaches the Park, double as an irrigation source for us, and — registered as an NSW RFS Static Water Supply — give the Park a firefighting water source on hand during a bushfire. That last point is why it's worth Council and the Park co-funding: it's a shared bushfire-response asset, not just a stormwater fix on our land.

Our opening position

Cost itemAmountOur proposed share
Road widening / resurfacing / footpath$1.2MNot a Cemetery cost — the road only narrows past our entrance, so this doesn't affect our visitors or operations
Parking meters$0.3MNot a Cemetery cost — a Council revenue line, commuter-driven, not cemetery-driven
Stormwater improvements$0.5MWe propose to fund and build a detention pond in place of this item, and invite Council/Park to co-fund a share (opening ask: ≤20%, ~$100K) given its bushfire and drainage value to both

We'd also like the three of us to jointly commission an independent hydrology assessment once the pond is operational, so we're all working from the same evidence on any run-off still reaching the Park, rather than trading assumptions.

What we're asking of Council and National Park

  • Council: confirm what state/RMS funding exists for the road and parking before we treat the full $2M as ours alone to cover.
  • National Park: consider registering the pond as a Static Water Supply once built, and share the monitoring data behind the stormwater claim so the joint hydrology work has a starting point.
  • Both: agree to the detention-pond concept in principle, and to co-funding it in recognition of its bushfire-water-supply and drainage value — not as an admission on our part about the pollution's source.

Process

On 29 July we'd like to settle the cost-sharing principle and the detention-pond concept, and leave detailed design and timing to a short follow-up. We're ready to move off this position — it's a starting point, not a final offer.

Our fallback range Supporting detail

Full detail on the Preparation page. In short: our ceiling was ~40–50% of the pond project cost (~$200–250K), conditional on design input and the irrigation/bushfire-storage value being locked in — and we opened well below it, at ≤20%.
