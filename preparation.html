What we had going for us

  • Council could eventually have forced us to upgrade our own stormwater at our own cost — better to share it three ways, or get ahead of it.

  • The same pond can register as an NSW RFS Static Water Supply — real firefighting water for the Park.

Negotiation prep worksheet

The full working-out behind our position — interests, options, standards and BATNA, both parties. Useful if the room wants the detail; the headline is the range above, which is what we actually walked in with.

Our interests

  • Minimise our share of the $0.5M stormwater cost.
  • Avoid being cast as the sole polluter — protect our standing with the community we serve.
  • Keep the Cemetery open and accessible throughout any works.
  • Turn the fix into a benefit where we could — cutting water costs through irrigation, and potentially offering bushfire-water storage.
  • Keep a workable long-term relationship with Council and the Park — we're neighbours for good.

Their interests inferred

Council — stay within capital budget; manage political exposure from bushfire risk and unregulated parking; avoid a costly legal dispute over drainage responsibility; be seen to broker a fair three-way deal.

National Park — reduce bushfire evacuation risk urgently; protect Park ecology from pollution; keep its own capital contribution low, since the road and parking problems aren't of its making.

Options creative, for us to bring

  1. Propose funding an on-site detention pond in place of the standard stormwater fix, and ask Council/Park to share its cost — not the original $0.5M line item, a better answer to it.
  2. The pond weakens the Park's pollution claim without settling it: it takes our own run-off out of the picture, and any pollution still reaching the Park afterward points to the road — Council's responsibility, not ours.
  3. Register that pond as an NSW RFS Static Water Supply — directly answers the Park's bushfire-evacuation concern, not just its pollution one, which is what makes a three-way contribution easier for them to justify.
  4. Independent hydrology assessment, ideally timed after the pond is in — the cleanest way to settle how much of any remaining run-off is Cemetery versus road.
  5. Staged works so Cemetery operations and access aren't disrupted.
  6. Support Council's parking-meter revenue plan in exchange for a lower stormwater share — support costs us nothing; we're not paying toward the meters either way.
  7. Joint pitch for RMS/state funding on the road and parking, since the rail station — not the Cemetery — created that demand.

Standards fairness benchmarks

  • "Polluter pays" — but proportional to actual, evidenced contribution, not assumption.
  • "Beneficiary pays" — road widening and parking meters mainly benefit the Park and Council, not us. The road only narrows and deteriorates past our entrance, so widening and footpath works there don't affect Cemetery visitors or operations.
  • Parking meters are a Council revenue line, not a shared community cost — we treated any contribution there as off the table across our whole range, not just as an opening position we might trade away.
  • Independent technical evidence (hydrology) over any party's say-so, including ours.
  • Comparable precedent for three-way stormwater cost-sharing between adjoining landowners and Council.

Our BATNA

If no deal was reached, our fallback was Council eventually compelling us to fund and build our own stormwater fix — via development-consent conditions or a clean-up notice under the Protection of the Environment Operations Act 1997 (NSW). That meant 100% of the cost, no design input, on their timeline.

We'd rather get ahead of that: self-fund and design the pond ourselves before we're compelled to, keeping the irrigation and possible bushfire-storage value we'd otherwise lose. It's not fully unilateral — on-site works still need Council's development consent — so it sidesteps the three-way cost split, not Council altogether.

Their BATNA assumed

Council — delay the works, absorb the political risk, chase grant funding elsewhere, or use regulatory powers to push the cost onto us.

National Park — keep escalating through RMS or other agencies; fund park-side mitigation alone without the full road fix.

From worksheet to letter Supporting detail

What made it into the letter — and what didn't

The worksheet above was the full working-out. The letter we actually sent (see Proposal) was shorter and more relational than that — on purpose. Here's the detail behind the one-line version on the previous page.

Made the cut

  • The detention pond itself, funded in kind or financially — our core proposal, exactly as we'd scoped it.
  • The NSW RFS Static Water Supply angle — our strongest shared-benefit argument, kept front and centre as the reason for Council and the Park to co-fund at all.
  • Parking meters as a flat non-negotiable — if anything, sharper in the final letter ("we will not consider sharing costs") than our draft standard of just treating it as off the table.
  • A relational, not adversarial, framing — leading with our long-standing relationship with Council and the Park and a "mutually beneficial outcome," rather than opening on who's to blame.

Left on the cutting-room floor

  • Our specific opening ask (≤20%, ~$100K). We opened on "open to discussing viable alternatives" instead of a number — keeping our actual range in reserve for the room rather than anchoring on paper.
  • The joint hydrology assessment ask. Dropped from the letter; still ours to raise live on 29 July if the pollution-source question comes up.
  • Asking Council to confirm state/RMS funding, and asking the Park to share its monitoring data. Both felt more suited to a live back-and-forth than an opening letter.
  • The itemised cost table and the "settle it by 29 July, details later" process paragraph. The final letter reads shorter and more relational than our worksheet draft — the numbers stayed in our own preparation, not on the page we sent.
24–26 July, group discussion Supporting detail

How our position took shape

The worksheet above didn't turn into the letter we sent in one step. Here's the actual back-and-forth between us that got it there — who proposed what, and why some of it didn't make the final cut.

  1. 24 Jul, 10:11 — Merryn — First pass at the worksheet: argued we're not responsible for the run-off past our entrance, floated funding an on-site detention pond as a way to remove ourselves from the roadworks negotiation altogether, and flagged parking meters as pure Council revenue with no case for a shared cost.
  2. 24 Jul, 13:31–13:53 — Merryn & Istvan — Sharpened the BATNA. Building the pond ourselves isn't the fallback — it's our preferred way of meeting the real fallback (Council eventually compelling us to fix our own stormwater at full cost, no design say) on our own terms. Also agreed the pond is leverage, not a knock-out blow: if the Park still sees pollution after it's built, that points back to the road, not us — which is why an independent hydrology assessment stayed in our options, timed after the pond goes in.
  3. 26 Jul, 11:40 — Merryn — First full draft of the actual position under Position / Proposal / Negotiables / Non-negotiables headings — this became the shape of the letter we sent, though at this point she flagged reluctance about committing to any stormwater cost-share at all.
  4. 26 Jul, 12:22 — Istvan — Circulated a fuller alternative draft: a named opening ask (≤20%, roughly $100K), an itemised cost table, specific asks of Council (confirm state/RMS funding) and the Park (share monitoring data, help register the pond), and a process paragraph for 29 July.
  5. 26 Jul, 12:45 — Istvan — Noted that National Park's own opening proposal didn't raise the stormwater/pollution issue at all — read as them under-using the brief, and, in our view, leaving less pressure on our position than we'd expected going in.
  6. 26 Jul, 13:48–13:56 — Istvan & Merryn — Agreed to use Merryn's shorter, more relational structure as the base letter, folding in the pond/RFS/monitoring reasoning from Istvan's draft without the dollar figures or itemised asks — those stayed in our own preparation rather than going out on paper.
  7. 26 Jul, 15:33–16:30 — Istvan & Merryn — Decided against a formal letterhead. Reasoned an informal, relational tone — matching how National Park had replied — was more likely to keep Council and the Park open to talking than a formal, authoritative one; the casual opening on the sent letter was a deliberate call, not an oversight.

See what made it into the letter — and what didn't above for how these calls actually landed on the page, and what we'd reconsider now that Council's and National Park's replies are in too.

Reserve positions Supporting detail

What we planned to hold back unless talks stalled, or to use as a closing sweetener.

  • We were willing to go as high as ~50% of the pond/stormwater cost if the detention pond/irrigation design was locked in — held back until later in the session.
  • We could offer an easement across Cemetery land for drainage works as goodwill if the numbers stalled.
  • We suspected Council had state/RMS grant funding for the road and parking it hadn't mentioned — worth probing before we moved off our opening number.
  • No independent hydrology evidence existed yet — our plan was to propose commissioning it jointly, ideally after the pond was built, so it measured what was left rather than the whole problem. Buys time and shifts the "sole polluter" framing without rejecting it outright.
  • The NSW RFS Static Water Supply program is real — dams and tanks over 3,000L already qualify — so we could point to it as independent backing that a Cemetery pond has genuine bushfire-response value, not just our own claim.
27–28 July Supporting detail

A brief of our own for the day

The worksheet above is our working-out. It's thorough, but nobody can use it live with seven other people on a Zoom call, so in the days before the session we pulled the position into a short brief of our own to take into the room — not to be confused with the case brief we were handed at the start, which is on the Case page.

It runs in two halves. The first is a read-through: where things stand, what we're after, our read on Council's and National Park's letters, the pond, and our fallback. The second is built to be glanced at mid-negotiation — an opening script, the Breakthrough Strategy moves mapped onto this particular room, a table of likely lines and our responses, a suggested range, and a checklist of what we needed agreed before we logged off.

Drafted, not agreed — and internal. It went round as a draft for the three of us to argue over and amend before Wednesday; the figures in it were suggestions, not a signed-off position. It also isn't published here and wasn't shared with Council or National Park, since it carries our walk-away thinking and the numbers we deliberately kept out of the letter we sent on 26 July.
