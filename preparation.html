Negotiation prep worksheet

The working-out behind the range above — interests, options, standards and BATNA.

Our interests

  • Minimise our share of the $0.5M stormwater cost.
  • Avoid being cast as the sole polluter — protect our standing with the community we serve.
  • Keep the Cemetery open and accessible throughout any works.
  • Turn the fix into a benefit: cheaper irrigation water, and bushfire storage we can offer.
  • Keep a workable long-term relationship with Council and the Park — we're neighbours for good.

Their interests inferred

Council — stay within capital budget; manage political exposure from bushfire risk and unregulated parking; avoid a legal fight over drainage responsibility.

National Park — reduce bushfire evacuation risk urgently; protect Park ecology; keep its own contribution low, since the road and parking problems aren't of its making.

Options ours to bring

  1. Fund an on-site detention pond in place of the standard stormwater fix, and ask Council and the Park to share its cost — a better answer to the $0.5M line, not the line itself.
  2. Register the pond as an NSW RFS Static Water Supply, so it answers the Park's bushfire concern too — that's what makes co-funding easy for them to justify.
  3. Independent hydrology assessment, timed after the pond goes in, to settle how much remaining run-off is road versus Cemetery.
  4. Stage the works so Cemetery access isn't disrupted.
  5. Support Council's parking-meter revenue plan in exchange for a lower stormwater share — support costs us nothing.

Standards fairness benchmarks

  • "Polluter pays" — but proportional to evidenced contribution, not assumption.
  • "Beneficiary pays" — the road only deteriorates past our entrance, so widening and footpath works there don't serve Cemetery visitors.
  • Parking meters are a Council revenue line, not a shared community cost.
  • Independent technical evidence over any party's say-so, including ours.

Our BATNA

No deal meant Council eventually compelling us to fund and build our own stormwater fix — through consent conditions or a clean-up notice under the Protection of the Environment Operations Act 1997 (NSW). That's 100% of the cost, no design input, on their timeline.

We'd rather get ahead of it: self-fund and design the pond before we're compelled to, keeping the irrigation and bushfire-storage value we'd otherwise lose.

Their BATNA assumed

Council — delay the works, absorb the political risk, chase grant funding, or use regulatory powers to push the cost onto us.

National Park — keep escalating through other agencies; fund park-side mitigation alone without the full road fix.

Held in reserve
  • Our ~50% ceiling, conditional on the pond design being locked in — not to be shown until late in the session.
  • An easement across Cemetery land for drainage works, as a closing sweetener.
  • A suspicion that Council had state/RMS grant funding it hadn't mentioned — worth probing before we moved off our opening.
24–26 July

How our position took shape

The worksheet didn't become the letter in one step.

  1. The pond as an exit, not a concession. Merryn's first pass argued we aren't responsible for run-off past our entrance, and floated funding a pond as a way out of the roadworks negotiation altogether.
  2. The BATNA got sharper. Building the pond ourselves isn't the fallback — it's our preferred way of meeting the real fallback (being compelled to fix our own stormwater at full cost, with no design say) on our own terms.
  3. Two drafts, one letter. Istvan's draft named an opening figure (~$100K) with an itemised cost table and specific asks. Merryn's was shorter and relational. We used hers as the base and folded in the pond/RFS reasoning — the dollar figures stayed in our own preparation.
  4. No letterhead, on purpose. An informal tone, matching how the Park had written to us, looked more likely to keep everyone talking than a formal one.
