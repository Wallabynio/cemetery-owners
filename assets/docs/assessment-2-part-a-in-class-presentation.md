# Assessment 2 | Part A: In-class presentation

**Due:** 5 Aug by 18:00

## Intent

This assignment provides you with the opportunity to "get to grips" with a practical example of development negotiation and critically reflect on the pros and cons of the experience. This is an opportunity to apply your experience and knowledge acquired in this course to a real-world negotiation.

## Objective

- Enable reflective practice on your personal views and values, and interpret how they might affect your professional judgement.
- Demonstrate ethical responsibilities of professional planners, and critically evaluate the ethical implications of complex problems.
- Communicate with people with a wide variety of cultural, social, economic and political perspectives using verbal, written and visual media.
- Determine sources of conflict, and apply conflict negotiation strategies appropriately.
- Develop coherent and logically structured arguments that use evidence appropriately.

## Due date

Week 5, Wednesday (completed in Zoom session from 6:00pm-7:30pm, Sydney time).

## Weighting

15%

## Task instructions

The class will be organised into groups, and each member of the group will be assigned a role (such as developer, community member, council or agency employee) in a development negotiation that will be explained and assigned to you.

Refer to the *Four Scenarios* document, which outlines the four case studies. Once you know your assigned case study (via *Groups*), open the document and review the relevant scenario and stakeholder information.

You will be allocated time to undertake a negotiation to solve a planning problem and attempt to reach a fair and equitable agreement between parties.

Each group will present to the class, explaining the process and outcomes of its negotiation, what techniques and processes worked well, what didn't, and why.

**Length:** 15 minutes per group.

## Rubric

### Criterion 1 - Quality of the group's presentation, particularly in regard to structure, clarity and fluency

| Rating | Description |
|---|---|
| **High Distinction** | The presentation is excellently structured, has a very logical flow |
| **Distinction** | The presentation is well structured, has a mostly logical flow |
| **Credit** | The presentation is quite well structured, has a generally logical flow |
| **Pass** | The presentation is structured, and has some flow although this could be more logical |
| **Fail** | The presentation is poorly structured, and does not have a logical flow |

### Criterion 2 - Demonstrated capacity to effectively negotiate in a group and achieve fair and equitable outcomes

| Rating | Description |
|---|---|
| **High Distinction** | Group demonstrates excellent negotiation strategies in the group context which leads to fair and equitable outcomes |
| **Distinction** | Group demonstrates very good negotiation strategies in the group context which leads to mostly fair and equitable outcomes |
| **Credit** | Group demonstrates good negotiation strategies in the group context which leads to generally fair and equitable outcomes |
| **Pass** | Group demonstrates some negotiation strategies in the group context which leads to adequate outcomes |
| **Fail** | Group does not demonstrate negotiation strategies in the group context which does not lead to fair and equitable outcomes |
